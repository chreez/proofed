## Scaling Behavior Analysis — Tartine Baguette

### Ingredient Classification

| Ingredient | ID | Amount | Classification | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All-Purpose Flour | ap_flour | 1070g | linear | Base structural component, baker's math |
| Bread Flour | bread_flour | 350g | linear | Protein contribution scales with total flour |
| Water | water | 920g | linear | Hydration ratio must remain constant |
| Active Dry Yeast | yeast | 3g | non_linear | Poolish yeast at 2x could be reduced ~20% since larger poolish mass ferments faster. At 0.5x keep proportional. |
| Mature Starter | starter | 15g | non_linear | Leaven seed amount: at 2x, 15g still seeds 440g leaven adequately — no need to double. At 0.5x, reduce to ~10g. |
| Salt | salt | 24g | linear | 2% baker's percentage must be maintained |

### Tested Multiplier Range
- **Min**: 0.5x (1-2 small baguettes — still enough dough to shape properly)
- **Max**: 1.5x (4-5 baguettes — limited by oven/stone capacity)

### Process Caveats
1. **Oven load**: A standard home baking stone fits 2-3 baguettes max. At 1.5x, you must bake in 2 batches. Second batch continues proofing while first bakes — risk of over-proofing.
2. **Bulk fermentation timing**: Larger dough mass (1.5x) generates more metabolic heat — bulk may complete 30-60 min faster. Use visual cues (20-30% volume increase, jiggle test) not clock time.
3. **Steam capacity**: More baguettes = more steam needed. The wet towel method may need an additional towel at 1.5x. At 0.5x, standard setup is fine.
4. **Shaping complexity**: More baguettes means more time during shaping — later pieces may over-proof while first ones are shaped. Work quickly or shape in pairs.
5. **Poolish/leaven timing**: At 0.5x, the pre-ferments are very small and may dry out or stall. Use a smaller, taller container to maintain humidity.
6. **Couche space**: Each baguette needs ~8cm of couche width. At 1.5x, ensure adequate couche length or use two couche setups.

### Sources
1. Tartine Bread by Chad Robertson — original formula and method (pp. 128-130)
2. The Fresh Loaf forums — community scaling experiences for artisan bread
3. Baker's percentage reference — flour-based scaling principles
