## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
Stack on top of Experiment 1's winning uncovered temperature. After the second loaf finishes its uncovered bake, turn the oven OFF, transfer both loaves to the middle rack (or leave in place), crack the oven door open ~2 inches with a folded potholder, and let them cool there for 10-15 minutes before moving to a wire rack for the remainder of the cooling hour.

## Hypothesis

Finish-cold-in-oven (door cracked, oven off) preserves crust crispness longer than immediate wire-rack cooling, because migrating internal moisture evaporates into the warm oven air instead of condensing on the crust in cool kitchen air. Measurable improvement at the 2hr and 6hr marks.

Source rationale: King Arthur Baking explicit recommendation ("Keep your baking crispy") + corroborating threads on The Fresh Loaf.

## Variables

**Changed:** Post-bake cooling → oven off + door cracked 2" for 10-15 min, then wire rack (vs. immediate wire-rack cool in Experiment 1)

**Held constant:**
- Uncovered bake temperature: whatever won in PF-178.2 (likely 475°F if E1 passes, else 450°F)
- All pre-bake variables identical to Experiment 1
- 500°F preheat, 450°F covered bake 22 min
- Uncovered duration 18 min (9 + flip + 9)
- Pull at 210°F internal

## Measurement rubric (same as PF-178.2)

- **Crust thickness** (thin / medium / thick)
- **Crust crispness** (1-5, when first cut ~90 min after bake)
- **Crust crispness retention at 2hr** (1-5)
- **Crust crispness retention at 6hr** (1-5)
- **Crumb quality** (pass/fail — watch for gummy/damp crumb from trapped moisture)
- **Overall preference** vs. Experiment 1

Log all 6 metrics in the cook_log entry referencing PF-178.3.

## Pass criteria

- Crispness retention at 2hr improves by ≥1 point vs. Experiment 1
- OR crispness retention at 6hr improves by ≥1 point vs. Experiment 1
- Crumb quality pass (no dampness from trapped moisture)

## Fail criteria

- No measurable difference vs. wire-rack cool
- Crumb becomes gummy / damp from slow cool
- Crust goes leathery (over-dried in residual heat)
