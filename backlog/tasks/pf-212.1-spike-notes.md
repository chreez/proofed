## 1. Tradition Survey (7 Traditions)

### 1a. Chinese Scallion Flower Rolls (Hua Juan, 花卷)

**Origin:** Northern China, staple breakfast/meal bread. Steamed, not baked.

**Rolling technique:** Dough rolled into a large rectangle (~23x45cm). Entire surface brushed with a thin layer of oil, then filling scattered evenly: finely chopped scallions, salt, five-spice powder or ground Sichuan pepper. Dough folded lengthwise in thirds (like a letter) to create layered strip.

**Filling distribution:** Oil acts as both flavor carrier and layer separator. The tri-fold creates 3 distinct filling layers. Hot oil is sometimes poured over scallions before applying to release more aroma.

**Sizing/portioning:** Folded strip cut crosswise into 16 equal strips. Two strips stacked, pressed together with chopstick indentation, then stretched and twisted into knot shapes. Yield: 8 buns from one batch.

**Key insight for our buns:** The oil-as-separator principle is directly relevant. Our melted butter filling serves the same function: it creates flaky, separable layers within the swirl while carrying the garlic flavor. The Chinese approach of coating the *entire* surface before adding aromatics ensures no dry spots.

**Sources:**
- [The Woks of Life — Hua Juan Flower Rolls](https://thewoksoflife.com/hua-juan-flower-rolls-scallion-buns/)
- [Red House Spice — Chinese Flower Rolls](https://redhousespice.com/scallion-flower-rolls/)

### 1b. Swedish Cardamom Buns (Kardemummabullar)

**Origin:** Sweden, central to fika coffee culture.

**Rolling technique:** Enriched dough rolled into a rectangle. Filling (butter-sugar-cardamom paste) spread on bottom half, top half folded down, then rolled a few times to flatten and adhere. Cut into vertical strips. Each strip slit up the center (creating "pants"), then each leg twisted and wrapped around the top, secured through center. Creates a distinctive knot/rosette shape.

**Filling distribution:** Paste spread on one half only, then fold-and-roll creates even lamination. The twist-and-knot shaping exposes filling layers on the outside surface.

**Sizing/portioning:** Strips cut to consistent width from the folded-and-rolled slab. Individual shaping (twist/knot) makes weight variance more forgiving since each bun is hand-shaped.

**Key insight for our buns:** The exposed-filling aesthetic is interesting but impractical for our gochujang buns baked in a cupcake tin. The knot shape would collapse into the tin wells. However, the principle of spreading filling on half the dough then folding could create denser filling concentration than full-surface spreading.

**Sources:**

### 1c. Babka (Eastern European/Jewish)

**Origin:** Eastern European Jewish tradition, originally sweet (chocolate, cinnamon). Savory variations increasingly popular (cheese-za'atar, pesto, everything-spice).

**Rolling technique:** Enriched dough rolled into a large rectangle (~23x46cm, about 0.6cm thick). Filling spread evenly leaving a small border. Rolled tightly from the long side into a log (~60cm). Log split lengthwise with bench knife, exposing filling layers. Two halves twisted around each other (braid) with cut sides facing up to display the layers.

**Filling distribution:** Even spread across entire surface minus border. The lengthwise split-and-twist creates dramatic exposed swirl pattern. Filling is visible from all angles.

**Sizing/portioning for individual buns (babka buns):** The twisted braid is cut at ~10cm intervals. Each segment folded in half (cut sides outward) and placed upright in muffin/popover pan wells. King Arthur recipe: 12x16" rectangle yields a 24" log, cut into six 4" segments. For 12 portions, double the recipe or use thinner slices.

**Key insight for our buns:** Babka buns in muffin tins are a proven format. The split-and-twist technique is more complex than a simple cinnamon-roll slice but creates a more dramatic visual. For gochujang buns, the extra handling might overwork the dough. The simple swirl slice is more reliable for consistent results with our enriched dough.

**Sources:**

### 1d. Korean Cream Cheese Garlic Bread (크림치즈 마늘빵)

**Origin:** Korean bakery trend, enormous viral popularity. Not a traditional swirl — uses a star/petal cut technique.

**Rolling technique:** Dough shaped into individual round buns (not rolled from a log). After baking, each bun is cut in a 6-way star pattern (like pizza slices) without cutting all the way through. Garlic cream cheese filling piped into each slit using a piping bag.

**Filling distribution:** Post-bake piping ensures filling reaches the center of each bun. The slits open during filling and the bread absorbs the garlic butter mixture. Buns are then dipped in or brushed with garlic butter sauce.

**Sizing/portioning:** Individual buns portioned before baking (~60-80g each), so sizing is straightforward.

**Key insight for our buns:** The post-bake filling approach is fundamentally different from our roll-up-with-filling method. However, the concept of a garlic butter *dip* after baking is worth noting — our honey-soy glaze already serves this function. The Korean bread confirms that garlic + butter + enriched dough is a proven flavor combination across cultures.

**Sources:**

### 1e. Turkish Gul Boregi (Rose Borek)

**Origin:** Ottoman/Turkish pastry tradition. "Gul" means rose — individual spiral pastries.

**Rolling technique:** Thin dough (yufka/filo-like) brushed with egg mixture or oil, filling placed along one edge, rolled into a snake/rope shape, then coiled into a tight spiral (like a snail shell). Each spiral is an individual portion placed in a round pan.

**Filling distribution:** Filling concentrated along one edge before rolling means the spiral has filling running through it in a continuous line rather than across the full surface. The egg/oil wash between layers prevents sticking and creates flaky separation.

**Sizing/portioning:** Each spiral is made from one sheet of dough — self-portioning. Size determined by dough sheet dimensions.

**Key insight for our buns:** The coiled-spiral shape (as opposed to our cut-from-log approach) would be an interesting alternative. However, coiling requires thin, extensible dough. Our enriched tangzhong dough is too thick and elastic for this technique. The log-slice method is better suited to our dough characteristics.

**Sources:**

### 1f. Japanese Milk Bread Rolls (Tangzhong/Shokupan)

**Origin:** Japan (yukone method), popularized across Asia by Taiwanese author Yvonne Chen. The tangzhong technique our recipe already uses.

**Rolling technique:** Individual portions (~60-80g) flattened into small rectangles (~13x20cm), short ends folded inward like a letter, flattened again, then rolled into a tight log from the short end. Creates a layered, self-contained roll.

**Filling distribution (savory variants):** Filling spread on the rectangle before folding and rolling. The fold-roll-fold-roll sequence creates multiple filling layers in a compact bun. Some savory versions use furikake, scallion, or curry paste.

**Sizing/portioning:** Pre-portioned into individual pieces before shaping. Each piece shaped independently, so no cutting-from-log is needed.

**Key insight for our buns:** Our dough already uses tangzhong. The Japanese individual-roll technique (shape each bun separately from a pre-portioned piece) avoids the uniformity problem of slicing a single log. However, it would require spreading filling on 12 small pieces of dough individually, which is significantly more labor-intensive than one big roll-and-slice operation.

### 1g. American Cinnamon Rolls (Standard Reference)

**Origin:** American baking, the direct ancestor of our swirl technique.

**Rolling technique:** Enriched dough rolled into a rectangle (typically 30x40cm for 12 rolls). Butter spread across entire surface, sugar-cinnamon mixture scattered evenly. Rolled tightly from the long side into a firm cylinder, seam pinched and ends sealed.

**Filling distribution:** Butter spread first (acts as glue and layer separator), then dry filling scattered on top. Rolling from the long side creates more swirl layers than rolling from the short side. A firm, taut roll prevents gaps.

**Sizing/portioning:** Log cut into 12 equal pieces. Best methods for uniformity:
1. **Geometric halving:** Cut in half, each half in half, each quarter into thirds = 12 even pieces
2. **Dental floss:** Slide unflavored floss under log, cross ends over top, pull tight. Zero compression, preserves spiral shape
3. **Weight-based:** Weigh total log, divide by 12, weigh each slice

**Key insight for our buns:** This is the direct technique our bake #3 used. The cinnamon roll method is the most practical path for our v3 rewrite: single rectangle, full-surface filling, roll from long side, slice into 12. The dental floss cutting method should be noted in the recipe for clean, uniform slices.

**Sources:**
- [King Arthur Baking — Dental Floss Trick](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2018/11/21/simple-cinnamon-roll-trick)
- [Voyo Eats — Uniform Cinnamon Rolls](https://voyoeats.com/how-to-achieve-uniform-size-in-every-cinnamon-roll/)

## 2. Sophie Bamford Comparison

### Recipe: Cheesy Gochujang & Spring Onion Swirls

Published October 2024 on Substack as part of GBBO 2024 Bake Along (Bread Week). Makes 12 swirls.

### Filling Composition

| Component | Sophie Bamford | Our Bake #3 |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| Gochujang in dough | No — dough is plain tangzhong | Yes — 30g in dough |
| Gochujang as filling layer | Yes — thin layer spread across surface | No |
| Fat in filling | None (oil/butter in dough only) | Melted butter in roll-up |
| Cheese | 150g mozzarella + 100g mature cheddar | None |
| Aromatics | 50g chopped spring onions | ~25g minced confit garlic |
| Glaze | Honey + soy + sesame seeds | Honey + soy + sesame seeds |

### Key Technique Differences

1. **Gochujang placement:** Sophie spreads gochujang as a *filling layer* on plain dough. Ours is mixed *into* the dough itself. This is a fundamental difference — Sophie's approach creates visible red swirl bands, while ours gives uniform color throughout the crumb.

2. **Rolling dimensions:** Sophie rolls to a 14x14" (~36x36cm) square. This is a square, not a rectangle, which creates a shorter, fatter log with fewer spiral turns. Our approach (rectangle, longer side) would create more spiral layers.

3. **Filling spread:** Sophie uses a palette knife for the gochujang layer, then scatters cheese and spring onions on top. Leaves 1" (2.5cm) border on the near edge for sealing. Fillings pressed down lightly before rolling.

4. **Rolling direction:** Sophie rolls from the far side toward the near side (away-to-you). Standard cinnamon roll direction.

5. **Cutting:** 1.5" (3.8cm) slices after trimming ends. Baked in muffin tin.

6. **Dough:** Uses tangzhong (same as ours) with 500g strong flour, plus gochujang and sesame oil in the dough. Her total dough flour is higher than ours (500g+30g tangzhong vs our 400g+20g).

### Comparison Notes (from user)

- **Gochujang as filling layer** is a different approach. Could be layered *on top of* our dough (which already has gochujang in it) for double gochujang intensity. This is a future variant idea, not v3 scope.
- **Shredded cheese in swirl** gives different melt/distribution behavior than brie-stuffed variant tested in bake #2. Worth future exploration.
- **Sophie's honey-soy glaze** is identical to ours — confirming this is the community-standard finish for gochujang buns.

**Sources:**
- [Sophie Bamford — Cheesy Gochujang & Spring Onion Swirls (Substack)](https://sophiebamford.substack.com/p/cheesy-gochujang-and-spring-onion)
- [K-Recipes — Sophie Bamford Recipe Adaptation](https://www.k-recipes.com/cheesy-gochujang-spring-onion-swirls/)

## 3. Alternative Shape Evaluation (4 Shapes)

### 3a. Babka Twist (Split-and-Braid)

**Technique:** Roll dough into rectangle, spread filling, roll into log. Split log lengthwise with bench knife. Twist the two halves around each other with cut sides up. Cut twisted braid into individual portions, fold each in half, place in muffin wells.

**Feasibility with our dough:** MODERATE. Our enriched tangzhong dough is soft enough to split and twist, but the extra handling risks degassing. The confit garlic pieces could tear the dough during the lengthwise split. The babka twist is visually dramatic but adds 5+ minutes of shaping time and requires more skill.

**Pros:**
- Dramatic visual (exposed swirl layers)
- Proven in muffin tin format (King Arthur recipe)
- More surface area for glaze absorption

**Cons:**
- Extra shaping step (split + twist + fold)
- Garlic pieces could cause tearing during split
- Less uniform than simple slice
- Harder to achieve 12 even portions

**Verdict:** Not recommended for v3. Consider as a "fancy presentation" variant in the future.

---

### 3b. Pull-Apart Mini Buns

**Technique:** Instead of one large log sliced into 12, divide filled dough into small pieces (~20-25g each), toss pieces in garlic butter, stack 3-4 pieces per muffin cup. Creates a monkey-bread-like pull-apart bun.

**Feasibility with our dough:** HIGH. Our dough handles well in small pieces. The garlic butter coating would replace the roll-up filling method.

**Pros:**
- Maximum glaze/butter absorption (huge surface area)
- Forgiving — no precision cutting needed
- Fun pull-apart texture

**Cons:**
- Loses the swirl visual entirely (the user explicitly wants swirl shape)
- Multiple small pieces = more handling time
- Doesn't leverage the roll-up technique that won bake #3 A/B test
- Dough pieces might not hold together as well

**Verdict:** Not recommended for v3. The swirl shape won the A/B test; this abandons the winning format. Could be a fun future variant.

---

### 3c. Individual Coil/Snail

**Technique:** Divide dough into 12 portions. Roll each portion into a thin rope (~30cm). Flatten rope, spread filling along length, fold closed, then coil into a snail/spiral shape. Place each coil in muffin cup.

**Feasibility with our dough:** LOW-MODERATE. Rolling enriched dough into thin ropes is labor-intensive and risks tearing. 12 individual ropes = significantly more shaping time. Confit garlic pieces would make thin ropes lumpy and prone to breaking.

**Pros:**
- Individual portioning before shaping (inherently uniform weight)
- Each coil is self-contained
- Visible spiral pattern from top

**Cons:**
- Very labor-intensive (12 individual shaping operations)
- Enriched dough resists being rolled thin
- Garlic pieces would tear thin ropes
- Less efficient than single roll-and-slice

**Verdict:** Not recommended. Too labor-intensive and impractical with our garlic-studded dough.

---

### 3d. Double-Layer Swirl (Our Recommended Approach)

**Technique:** This is an enhanced version of the standard cinnamon roll method. Roll dough into a rectangle, spread melted butter + minced confit garlic across entire surface (leaving border at one short edge), roll tightly from the long side, slice into 12 even portions, place cut-side-up in muffin cups.

**Feasibility with our dough:** HIGH. This is exactly what worked in bake #3, refined for consistency. The technique is proven with our specific dough and ingredients.

**Pros:**
- Already validated in bake #3 A/B test
- Single roll-and-slice operation (efficient)
- Butter + garlic distributed across entire surface
- Cut-side-up displays the swirl pattern
- Standard technique with abundant guidance available
- Compatible with cupcake tin format

**Cons:**
- Requires careful cutting for uniform portions (solvable with technique)
- End pieces may be slightly less uniform (trim ends)

**Verdict:** RECOMMENDED for v3. See Section 4 for detailed technique specification.

## 4. V3 Technique Recommendation

### Dough Weight Estimate

Current recipe ingredients (dough portion only): bread flour 420g, water 100g, yeast 7g, milk 120g, butter 28g, sugar 40g, salt 5g, gochujang 30g, egg 50g. **Total raw dough: ~800g.** After bulk fermentation and folding in ~25g confit garlic, working weight is approximately **~825g.**

For 12 buns: ~69g per bun (pre-bake weight).

### Rolling Dimensions

**Target rectangle: 40cm x 30cm (16" x 12")**

- Roll from the **long side** (40cm) to create a log ~30cm (12") long
- A 30cm log cut into 12 pieces = 2.5cm (1") per slice
- 40cm width creates sufficient spiral turns (~5-6 visible layers per slice)
- This matches standard cinnamon roll proportions scaled for our dough weight

**Dough thickness:** Roll to approximately 0.6-0.8cm (1/4"-1/3") even thickness. The tangzhong dough is elastic; let it rest 2-3 minutes mid-roll if it springs back.

### Filling Spread Method

1. **Melt 1-2 tbsp (~15-28g) unsalted butter** and let cool slightly (not hot enough to activate yeast)
2. **Brush melted butter** across the entire rectangle surface using a pastry brush, leaving a **2.5cm (1") border on the far long edge** (this is the sealing edge)
3. **Scatter minced confit garlic** (~25g, ~6 cloves worth) evenly across the buttered surface
4. **Press garlic lightly** into the butter with fingertips so it adheres during rolling

**Note:** The confit garlic is already mixed into the dough (fold-in-garlic step). This filling layer is *additional* garlic for the swirl — it replaces the "added melted butter pat and minced garlic" from bake #3 notes. The v3 recipe should clarify that some garlic goes in the dough (for crumb flavor) and some goes in the roll-up (for swirl pockets).

### Rolling Technique

1. Starting from the **long edge nearest you**, begin rolling the dough away from you
2. Keep the roll **taut** — tuck the dough under itself as you go (like the ATK cinnamon bun technique)
3. Apply even, gentle pressure across the full width to prevent the log from tapering at the ends
4. When you reach the unsealed border, **pinch the seam firmly** along the entire length
5. Gently roll the sealed log back and forth once to even out diameter
6. **Rest the log seam-side-down** for 2 minutes before cutting — this helps the seam set

### Slice Count for 12 Even Portions

**Recommended method: Geometric halving + dental floss**

1. **Trim both ends** of the log (~1cm each) to remove the tapered/unsealed portions. (Baker's snack. ~15g total trim.)
2. **Mark the center** with a light knife score
3. **Cut in half** (dental floss or bench knife)
4. **Cut each half in half** = 4 equal pieces
5. **Cut each quarter into thirds** = 12 pieces

**Cutting tool:** Unflavored dental floss gives the cleanest cut on enriched dough without compressing the spiral. Slide floss under the log, cross the ends over the top, pull tight. If using a knife, use a sharp serrated knife with a gentle sawing motion — never press straight down.

**Weight check (optional):** Total log minus trims should be ~810g. Target: 67-68g per piece. Weigh the first few cuts to calibrate your eye.

### Seam Handling

- **Seam position when cutting:** Keep seam-side-down while cutting so gravity holds it closed
- **Seam position in tin:** Place slices **cut-side-up** in muffin cups. The swirl pattern should be visible from the top
- **If seam opens during cutting:** Press it closed gently. Once the bun is in the muffin cup, the tin walls hold everything in place during proofing and baking
- **Prevention:** A firm, taut roll with a well-pinched seam rarely opens. If filling leaks from the seam, it usually means the roll was too loose or there was too much butter

### Muffin Tin Placement

- Lightly grease each cup (butter or nonstick spray)
- Place each slice cut-side-up, pressing gently to make contact with the cup bottom
- The buns will expand during proofing to fill the cups and rise above the rim
- Cover loosely with plastic wrap for second rise

### Summary of Shape Stage Changes for V3

| Step | Current v1.2 | Proposed v3 |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| Fold in garlic | 25g folded into dough | Keep — but reduce to ~15g for dough, reserve ~10g for filling |
| Divide & shape | Divide into 75g pieces, pre-round | **Replace:** Roll into 40x30cm rectangle |
| Bench rest | 5 min rest | Keep — rest before rolling if dough springs back |
| Filling | None (garlic already in dough) | **New:** Brush melted butter, scatter confit garlic |
| Final shape | Roll into tight balls | **Replace:** Roll up from long side, pinch seam |
| Portion | Pre-portioned by weight | **Replace:** Trim ends, cut log into 12 with dental floss |
| Pan | Sheet pan with parchment | **Replace:** Greased muffin tin (12-cup) |
| Second rise | Rise on sheet pan | Rise in muffin tin |
| Score & wash | Score crosses on tops | **Remove scoring** (swirl tops don't need it); keep egg wash |

## 5. Future Variant Ideas (Out of V3 Scope)

These are promising directions surfaced by the research. Each cites the source tradition. None enter v3 — they are future draft candidates.

### 5a. Gochujang Filling Layer (Double Gochujang)

Spread a thin layer of gochujang paste on the rectangle *before* the butter, creating visible red swirl bands. Our dough already has gochujang in it; adding a filling layer would intensify the heat and create a two-tone swirl (orange crumb + red filling streaks).

**Source tradition:** Sophie Bamford's approach — she uses plain dough + gochujang filling. Combining both is unexplored territory.

### 5b. Shredded Cheese Swirl

Add 100-150g grated mozzarella + mature cheddar on top of the butter layer before rolling. Cheese melts into the swirl layers and creates a crispy, caramelized "skirt" around the base of each bun where it leaks from the muffin cup.

**Source tradition:** Sophie Bamford (mozzarella + cheddar); Edd Kimber's gochujang garlic bread buns (cheese + gochujang); Korean cream cheese garlic bread (cream cheese filling).

### 5c. Spring Onion / Scallion Addition

Add 30-50g finely chopped spring onions to the filling layer. User is currently on the fence about this.

**Source tradition:** Chinese hua juan (scallion is the primary filling); Sophie Bamford (spring onions are a key component).

### 5d. Sesame Oil in Filling

Replace or supplement the melted butter with toasted sesame oil (~1 tbsp) for a nutty, Korean-inflected filling. Could be mixed with the butter rather than replacing it.

**Source tradition:** Sophie Bamford uses sesame oil in the dough; Chinese scallion rolls use sesame oil as the filling fat; Korean garlic bread traditions often include sesame.

### 5e. Scallion Oil

Make a quick scallion oil (heat neutral oil, pour over chopped scallions) and use as the filling fat instead of plain butter. Deeper allium flavor than raw scallions.

**Source tradition:** Chinese scallion bread (zhima dabing) — hot oil over scallions releases more aroma. Multiple Chinese bread traditions use this technique.

### 5f. Babka Twist Presentation

For special occasions: split the filled log lengthwise and twist into a braid before slicing into individual portions. More dramatic visual at the cost of extra handling time.

**Source tradition:** Eastern European babka; King Arthur seeded babka buns (individual muffin tin format proven).

### 5g. Post-Bake Garlic Butter Dip

After glazing with honey-soy, dip or brush buns with an additional garlic butter (melted butter + minced confit garlic + garlic oil from confit). Maximum garlic impact.

**Source tradition:** Korean cream cheese garlic bread (garlic butter dip/soak is signature technique); Edd Kimber's gochujang garlic bread buns.

## 7. Rolling Out Enriched Dough — Troubleshooting

Research completed: 2026-04-22

Context: Bake #3 used a tangzhong-enriched dough (bread flour, butter, egg, milk, gochujang, sugar) that was tough and resistant when rolling to a 40x30cm rectangle for swirl shaping. This section collects specific, actionable fixes.

---

### 7a. Why Enriched Tangzhong Dough Fights Back

Three factors compound in our dough to make it resistant to rolling:

1. **Bread flour = high protein (12-14%).** More gluten protein means a stronger, more elastic network. Bread flour creates dough that "holds its shape better" but also "resists being rolled thin" compared to all-purpose flour (8-11% protein). Our recipe uses 420g bread flour — this is the primary driver of springback.

2. **Salt + gochujang tighten gluten.** Salt causes gluten strands to tighten and compact by neutralizing charged sites on the protein, reducing extensibility. Our dough has 5g salt *plus* ~30g gochujang, which contains additional salt (~6% sodium in typical gochujang). The combined salt load tightens the gluten network more than a standard cinnamon roll recipe. Gochujang also adds fermented soybean paste and sugars that increase dough stiffness. ([Cargill — Salt in Bread Dough](https://www.cargill.com/salt-in-perspective/salt-in-bread-dough); [PMC — NaCl Effect on Gluten](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9525553/))

3. **Tangzhong adds structure.** The pre-cooked flour paste gelatinizes starch, which absorbs and retains moisture. This makes the crumb soft after baking but can make the raw dough feel dense and resistant during shaping. The dough is "really soft" but paradoxically also "hard to work with" because the gelatinized starch creates a cohesive, clingy mass. ([The Flavor Bender — Hokkaido Milk Bread](https://www.theflavorbender.com/japanese-milk-bread-recipe-hokkaido-milk-bread/); [The Little Epicurean — Milk Bread Rolls](https://www.thelittleepicurean.com/milk-bread-rolls/))

**Bottom line:** This dough has *more* elastic resistance than typical cinnamon roll dough. Standard "just roll it out" instructions assume softer, lower-protein doughs. Our recipe needs explicit rest-and-roll guidance.

---

### 7b. The Fix: Staged Bench Rest (Most Important)

**The single most effective intervention is resting the dough before and during rolling.**

When you knead or mix enriched dough, gluten tightens into an elastic network that snaps back when stretched. Resting allows the gluten to relax, making the dough extensible rather than elastic. This is not optional for our dough — it's the difference between a frustrating fight and an easy roll-out.

**Protocol for our v3 recipe:**

| Step | Action | Duration |
|------|--------|----------|
| 1 | After bulk ferment, turn dough onto lightly greased surface | — |
| 2 | Pat into a rough rectangle with hands (~25x20cm) | 1-2 min |
| 3 | **Rest #1:** Cover with plastic wrap or inverted bowl | **15-20 min** |
| 4 | Roll from center outward toward 40x30cm. Stop if dough springs back. | 2-3 min |
| 5 | **Rest #2 (if needed):** Cover again | **10-15 min** |
| 6 | Finish rolling to 40x30cm, ~0.6-0.8cm thick | 1-2 min |

**Key principle:** If the dough springs back more than ~1cm after you lift the pin, stop and rest it. Forcing it creates tension that worsens the problem. Two short rests beat one long fight. ([The Fresh Loaf — Big Batches of Enriched Dough](https://www.thefreshloaf.com/node/74098/help-big-batches-enriched-dough-cinnamon-rolls); [Baking Forums — Overcoming Elastic Dough](https://www.baking-forums.com/threads/how-to-overcome-elastic-cinnamon-roll-dough.6254/); [King Arthur — How to Stretch Pizza Dough](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2024/02/14/how-to-stretch-pizza-dough))

---

### 7c. Temperature: Chill the Dough

Cold dough is dramatically easier to roll than room-temperature dough for tangzhong-enriched recipes.

- **Refrigerate after bulk ferment:** 30-60 minutes in the fridge before rolling. The cold firms up the butter, stiffens the dough slightly, and — counterintuitively — makes it *less* springy because cold gluten relaxes more slowly but also resists less aggressively. Cold dough holds its shape as you roll instead of snapping back.
- **Multiple tangzhong/milk bread sources recommend this:** "Because the dough can be really soft, chilling the dough for a few hours is highly recommended to make shaping much easier." Work quickly once cold dough comes out — "form the loaf or rolls quickly before it warms up."
- **Alternative: overnight cold retard.** After mixing, refrigerate the entire bulk ferment (8-12 hours). Roll out cold the next morning. This also develops more flavor. Many cinnamon roll recipes specifically call for overnight refrigeration because "dough rolls out easier and has better texture."

**Recommendation for v3:** Add a 30-60 min fridge rest after bulk ferment, before the roll-out step. Note this in the exit condition: "Dough chilled and pliable, not fighting the pin."

([The Flavor Bender — Hokkaido Milk Bread](https://www.theflavorbender.com/japanese-milk-bread-recipe-hokkaido-milk-bread/); [The Little Epicurean — Milk Bread Rolls](https://www.thelittleepicurean.com/milk-bread-rolls/); [Sally's Baking Addiction — Overnight Cinnamon Rolls](https://sallysbakingaddiction.com/overnight-cinnamon-rolls/); [Baker Bettie — Overnight Cinnamon Rolls](https://bakerbettie.com/overnight-cinnamon-rolls/))

---

### 7d. Rolling Technique (Step-by-Step)

These techniques specifically address getting enriched dough into a clean rectangle:

1. **Pre-shape with hands first.** Before touching the rolling pin, use your palms and fingertips to press and pat the dough into a rough rectangle. "Try patting a cube shape from a round blob of dough first, then roll out — there will be a big difference." This gives the pin a head start and prevents fighting a round mass into a rectangle. ([MasterClass — How to Roll Dough Evenly](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-roll-dough))

2. **Grease, don't flour.** King Arthur's Perfectly Pillowy Cinnamon Rolls specify a "lightly greased work surface" rather than flour. Flour on enriched dough can toughen the surface and make it grip the counter. A thin film of oil or butter spray lets the dough slide and stretch. ([King Arthur — Perfectly Pillowy Cinnamon Rolls](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/perfectly-pillowy-cinnamon-rolls-recipe))

3. **Roll from center outward.** Place the pin in the center of the dough and roll *away* from you, then return to center and roll *toward* you. Rotate 90 degrees and repeat. Never start from an edge — that creates uneven thickness and tears. ([MasterClass — How to Roll Dough Evenly](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/how-to-roll-dough); [DoughRollerMachine — How to Roll Dough Evenly](https://doughrollermachine.com/blogs/dough-sheeter-roller-machine-blog/how-to-roll-dough-evenly-for-pizza-pastry-bread-with-or-without-a-dough-sheeter))

4. **Roll diagonally to the corners.** After establishing the basic rectangle, roll diagonally from center toward each corner. This is the key to getting actual corners instead of rounded ovals. "Starting in the center, and pressing harder now, roll out diagonally towards the corners." ([DoughRollerMachine — Rolling Guide](https://doughrollermachine.com/blogs/dough-sheeter-roller-machine-blog/how-to-roll-dough-evenly-for-pizza-pastry-bread-with-or-without-a-dough-sheeter))

5. **Flip and rotate frequently.** Every 3-4 passes, lift the dough, rotate it 90 degrees, and flip it over. This prevents sticking, ensures even thickness, and lets you re-grease the surface if needed. Check for thin spots and thick spots each time you flip.

6. **Aim for actual corners.** "Try to pat the dough into an actual rectangle (with corners), rather than an oval." Having sharp corners ensures the rolled log is the same thickness across its entire length, which means all 12 slices will be uniform. ([King Arthur — Perfectly Pillowy Cinnamon Rolls](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/perfectly-pillowy-cinnamon-rolls-recipe))

7. **Even pressure, not heavy pressure.** Let the weight of the pin do the work. Pressing hard activates more gluten resistance. Light, repeated passes are more effective than one heavy push.

---

### 7e. Flour Type Consideration

Our recipe uses 100% bread flour (420g). This maximizes chew and structure but also maximizes rolling difficulty. Options to consider for v3:

| Approach | Flour Mix | Effect on Rolling | Effect on Crumb |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Current | 100% bread flour | Most resistant | Chewy, structured |
| Blend | 50/50 bread + AP | Noticeably easier | Softer, still good structure |
| All AP | 100% AP flour | Easiest to roll | Tender, less chew |

**Professional bakers often blend:** "Some pros use a mix of both flour types. The resulting rolls are less chewy and bready than those made with just bread flour, but lighter and fluffier than those made with just all-purpose flour." ([World's Best Cinnamon Rolls — Bread Flour](https://worldsbestcinnamonrolls.com/is-bread-flour-better-for-cinnamon-rolls/); [LipTouchFoods — Bread vs AP](https://liptouchfoods.com/is-bread-flour-or-all-purpose-flour-better-for-cinnamon-rolls/))

**Note:** Sophie Bamford uses 500g strong (bread) flour for her gochujang swirl buns, so 100% bread flour is a validated choice for this style. The rolling difficulty is a known trade-off. If the bench rest + chill protocol from sections 7b-7c solves the problem, there's no reason to change flour. Only consider blending if the dough remains unworkable after proper resting.

---

### 7f. What NOT to Do

Common bad advice and why it fails for our dough:

- **"Knead more to develop gluten"** — Wrong direction. Our dough already has strong gluten from bread flour + machine mixing. More kneading = more elasticity = more springback. The fix is *relaxation*, not more development.

- **"Add more flour while rolling"** — Tempting but counterproductive for enriched dough. Extra flour toughens the surface, creates dry spots in the swirl layers, and fights the tangzhong's moisture retention. Use a greased surface instead.

- **"Just use more force"** — Pressing harder activates gluten resistance. The dough fights back harder the harder you push. Light, patient passes with rests between are the answer.

- **"Skip the bench rest to save time"** — The bench rest IS the time-saver. A 15-minute rest means 2 minutes of easy rolling. Skipping it means 10 minutes of wrestling followed by an uneven rectangle that produces uneven buns.

---

### 7g. Gochujang-Specific Notes

How gochujang paste affects dough workability:

- **Additional salt:** Gochujang contains ~6% sodium. Our 30g of gochujang adds roughly 1.8g of additional salt to the dough, tightening gluten beyond what the 5g of added salt alone would do. Total effective salt is ~6.8g in the dough, which is on the higher end for this dough weight.

- **Sugar content:** Gochujang contains ~16% sugar (from fermented rice). This adds to the dough's sugar load, which can make gluten development slightly slower but doesn't specifically cause springback.

- **Fermented soybean protein:** Gochujang contains fermented soybean powder. The amino acids from fermentation can interact with gluten proteins, potentially adding to dough stiffness. This is not well-studied in bread science, but bakers working with miso and gochujang doughs anecdotally report stiffer-than-expected results.

- **Hydration contribution:** Gochujang is ~44% water. Our 30g adds ~13g of water to the dough, which marginally increases hydration. This is a minor factor.

**Net effect:** Gochujang makes the dough slightly tighter/stiffer than a plain tangzhong enriched dough of the same flour weight. The bench rest protocol is even more important because of this.

---

### 7h. V3 Recipe Recommendations

Based on this research, here are the specific changes for the v3 roll-out step:

1. **Add a fridge rest step** after bulk ferment: "Refrigerate dough 30-60 minutes until firm but pliable."

2. **Add explicit bench rest** before rolling: "Pat into a rough rectangle, cover, rest 15-20 minutes."

3. **Update exit condition** for the roll-out state: "Dough rolled to 40x30cm rectangle without springing back. If dough resists, cover and rest 10-15 minutes, then continue."

4. **Add step note** about rolling technique: "Roll from center outward on a lightly greased (not floured) surface. Roll diagonally toward corners for a true rectangle. Flip and rotate every 3-4 passes."

5. **Add step note** about patience: "Two 15-minute rests are faster than one long fight with elastic dough. If the dough springs back more than 1cm, stop and rest it."

6. **Keep bread flour** — the bench rest + chill protocol should solve the problem without changing the flour. Only consider a 50/50 blend if rolling remains difficult after proper resting.
