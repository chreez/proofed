## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
Head-to-head against the winning configuration from Experiments 1+2. Lower the uncovered bake temperature to 420°F and extend the uncovered duration to ~24 minutes (2× 12-min flips) to explore the lower-and-longer end of the spectrum. This is the explicit outstanding hypothesis from the PF-178 parent description.

## Hypothesis

Lowering uncovered temp to 420°F and extending to 24 min (2× 12-min flips) will produce a thicker, crunchier crust that's a distinct "crispy" texture vs. Experiment 1's thinner shattery crust. User ranks preference between the two styles to inform recipe lock-in.

## Variables

**Changed:** Uncovered bake phase → 420°F × 24 min (2× 12-min flips) vs. Experiment 1's 475°F × 18 min (2× 9-min flips)

**Held constant:**
- Everything else from the winning Experiment 1 + Experiment 2 configuration
- 500°F preheat, 450°F covered 22 min
- Finish-cold-in-oven cooling (assuming E2 passes — if it fails, revert to wire rack)
- Pull at 210°F internal
- Same dough params: 70% hydration, 100% bread flour, 20% inoculation, fermentolyse, cold proof overnight

## Measurement rubric (same as PF-178.2 and PF-178.3)

- **Crust thickness** (thin / medium / thick)
- **Crust crispness** (1-5) at first cut
- **Crust crispness retention at 2hr** (1-5)
- **Crust crispness retention at 6hr** (1-5)
- **Crumb quality** (pass/fail — watch for over-dried crumb from extended bake time)
- **Overall preference** — explicit ranking vs. Experiment 1 result

Log all 6 metrics in the cook_log entry referencing PF-178.4.

## Pass criteria

- Produces a distinctly thicker, still-crisp crust (not leathery)
- User can rank preference between this and Experiment 1 (either direction is a valid result)
- No crumb regression

## Fail criteria

- Crust becomes leathery or too hard to cut cleanly
- Crumb dries out from extended bake time
- Uncovered phase burns the bottom or creates uneven browning

## Decision output

After this experiment completes, the user picks the winning uncovered-phase configuration (higher-and-shorter vs. lower-and-longer) and PF-178 proceeds to the recipe lock-in phase (ACs #6-#9).
