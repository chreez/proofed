# Grain-Free Bread: Food Science and Technique Guide

Research compiled for a grain-free bread recipe using almond flour, coconut flour, and eggs -- no wheat, no yeast, no sugar.

---

## 1. The Science of Structure Without Gluten

### Why Almond Flour Bread Is Dense

In wheat bread, glutenin and gliadin proteins combine with water during kneading to form an elastic, stretchy network that functions like microscopic rubber bands holding air bubbles in place. Remove gluten and you remove the entire scaffolding system that traps gas and maintains structure during baking.

Almond flour compounds the problem in two ways:

1. **No protein network**: Almond proteins don't form extensible networks. They coagulate when heated (like egg protein) but don't stretch.
2. **Low absorption**: Almond flour is barely absorbent compared to wheat flour. It's essentially ground nuts -- high fat (around 50%), high protein (21%), but minimal starch. There's nothing to gelatinize and set into a crumb structure.
3. **Heavy and oily**: The high fat content weighs down any air you manage to incorporate. Fat coats proteins and inhibits foam formation.

The result: almond flour batters can't hold air during mixing, can't expand during baking, and collapse into dense, flat loaves.

### What Actually Provides Structure (Ranked by Importance)

1. **Eggs** -- The primary structural protein. Egg proteins denature and coagulate between 144-158F, setting into a rigid matrix. This is the backbone of grain-free bread. Most almond flour bread recipes require 4-6 eggs where wheat bread uses 0-2.

2. **Psyllium husk** -- Forms a gel network that mimics gluten's elasticity (see Section 2). This is the single most important additive for bread-like texture.

3. **Starch gelatinization** -- If using any starch (tapioca, potato, arrowroot), it undergoes a four-phase transformation during baking:
   - Absorption phase (140-160F): Granules absorb water
   - Swelling phase (160-180F): Granules expand
   - Bursting phase (180-212F): Cell walls rupture, releasing starch
   - Gel formation: Released starch creates crumb structure
   Internal temp must reach 200F+ for complete transformation.

4. **Chemical leavening** -- Baking soda + acid (ACV) produces CO2 gas. Without a strong protein/gel network to trap it, much of this gas escapes. The network must be established BEFORE the gas is produced.

5. **Whipped egg whites** -- Mechanical leavening that incorporates air directly into the batter. More reliable than chemical leavening in grain-free systems because the air is already trapped in the foam.

**Sources**: [NogluBread: Science of GF Bread](https://www.noglubread.com/science-gluten-free-dairy-free-bread/), [ScienceDirect: Psyllium as improver in GF bread](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0023643821013098), [PMC: Psyllium improves GF bread quality](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8145964/)

## 2. Psyllium Husk: The Gluten Mimic

### How It Works

Psyllium (seed husk of Plantago ovata) is a hydrocolloid -- it absorbs 8-14x its weight in water and forms a viscous, stretchy gel. The fiber contains mucilage that creates networks structurally similar to gluten.

The mechanism is well-documented in peer-reviewed research:

- **Gas holding capacity**: Incorporating psyllium increases gas holding capacity from 83.67% to 98.50% in GF dough. For comparison, wheat dough is ~99%.
- **Specific volume**: Psyllium increased loaf volume by approximately 50% vs control GF bread (1.41 cm3/g to 2.08 cm3/g). Still ~25% lower than wheat bread, but a dramatic improvement.
- **Crumb firmness**: Reduced by 65-75% vs control GF bread. Control GF bread was 8x firmer than wheat; with 17% psyllium, only 2-3x firmer.
- **Shelf life**: Delays staling significantly. After 72 hours, psyllium bread maintained acceptability scores of 6.8-8.3, comparable to wheat bread.
- **Mechanism**: Water-binding limits moisture mobility, dilutes starch concentration (reducing retrogradation), strengthens cell boundaries for better gas retention.

### Optimal Amounts

- **Baker's percentage**: 5.5-6% of total flour weight (5.5-6g per 100g flour blend)
- **By volume**: 1-2 teaspoons psyllium husk POWDER per cup of flour
- **Research optimal**: The PMC study found 17.14% (flour weight basis) was superior, but that's extremely high -- practical recipes land at 5-8%.
- **Pre-hydration**: Mix with warm liquid at 10:1 water-to-psyllium ratio and let gel for 5 minutes before adding to batter.

### Powder vs Whole Husks

**Critical distinction**: If using psyllium husk POWDER instead of whole husks, use HALF the amount. Powder is more finely ground, absorbs faster, and can make bread heavy/gummy if overdosed.

### Psyllium + Xanthan Gum Combo

A tested optimal ratio is **3 parts psyllium to 2 parts xanthan gum**. The psyllium provides elasticity and moisture retention; the xanthan provides gas-trapping film formation. Together they cover more of gluten's functions than either alone.

### Watch Out For

- **Brand variation**: Different psyllium brands have different absorption rates. Some cause a purple/grey tint in bread.
- **Color**: Psyllium can darken bread crumb. Not a flavor issue, but visual.
- **Over-hydration**: Too much psyllium = gummy, heavy bread that crumbles into "chunks of gummy crumbles" after 1-2 days.

**Sources**: [PMC: Psyllium improves GF bread quality](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8145964/), [Gluten Free Alchemist: Psyllium in GF Baking](https://www.glutenfreealchemist.com/psyllium-husk-in-gluten-free-baking-bread-baking/), [NogluBread: Science of GF Bread](https://www.noglubread.com/science-gluten-free-dairy-free-bread/), [Konsyl: Baking with Psyllium](https://konsyl.com/guide-to-baking-with-psyllium-husk-powder/), [The Loopy Whisk: Psyllium 101](https://theloopywhisk.com/2021/10/23/psyllium-husk-101/)

## 3. Crust Development Without Sugar

### The Challenge

Bread crust browning relies on two reactions:
1. **Maillard reaction**: Amino acids + reducing sugars at 280-330F. Produces flavor + brown color.
2. **Caramelization**: Sugars alone at 320-360F. Produces browning + bitter-sweet flavor.

With no added sugar and no wheat flour (which contains its own sugars from starch breakdown), both reactions are starved of substrate.

### Solutions for Grain-Free Bread

**Maillard reaction can still work** because it needs amino acids + reducing sugars, and we have both:
- **Amino acids**: Abundant in eggs and almond flour (21% protein). The protein in almond flour actually provides MORE free amino acids than wheat flour.
- **Reducing sugars**: Small amounts exist naturally in eggs, coconut flour, and almond flour. Not as much as wheat, but enough for some browning.

**Techniques to maximize browning**:

1. **Egg wash**: Brush the top with beaten egg yolk before baking. The yolk proteins and fats promote Maillard reaction at the surface. Gives golden-brown color with slight sheen.

2. **Higher initial temperature**: Start at 400-425F for the first 10-15 minutes, then reduce to 350F. The high initial heat promotes surface browning before the interior sets.

3. **Baking soda at the surface**: A very light dusting of baking soda on the crust raises surface pH to alkaline (pH 6-8 is optimal for Maillard). This is the pretzel principle. CAUTION: Too much baking soda makes the crust tough and soapy-tasting.

4. **Dairy protein**: If dairy is allowed, brushing with milk or buttermilk provides lactose (reducing sugar) and casein (protein) -- both excellent Maillard substrates.

5. **Steam in the first 10 minutes**: A pan of water in the oven creates a humid environment. Steam keeps the crust pliable longer, allowing more oven spring before it sets. Once steam is removed, the dry heat promotes rapid browning.

6. **Yogurt in the batter**: If dairy is allowed, yogurt aids browning AND provides acid for leavening. The lactic acid and milk proteins contribute to crust color.

**What WON'T work well**: Relying on baking soda alone for browning. Testing shows it turns the crust brown but also tough before the loaf is fully cooked through, and contributes a soapy taste to GF bread.

**Sources**: [King Arthur: Understanding the Maillard Reaction](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2022/02/15/understanding-the-maillard-reaction-in-baking), [BAKERpedia: Maillard Reaction](https://bakerpedia.com/processes/maillard-reaction/), [Oklahoma Wheat Commission: Bread Browning](https://okwheat.org/baking-blog/when-bread-wont-brown-common-causes-and-solutions/), [George Eats: GF Bread Without Yeast](https://georgeats.com/recipes/gluten-free-bread-without-yeast/)

## 4. Coconut Flour: The Hydration Problem

### Why It's So Thirsty

Coconut flour contains **60.9% total dietary fiber** (56.8% insoluble, 3.8% soluble). This is the highest fiber content of any baking flour. The fiber acts as a sponge -- and the flour's defatted, porous texture amplifies absorption.

Key numbers:
- Absorbs **4-5x its weight** in liquid
- Requires **3-4x more liquid** than wheat flour
- Substitution ratio: 1/4 cup coconut flour replaces 1 cup wheat flour
- For every 1/4 cup coconut flour, add at least 1/4 cup extra liquid
- Add 1 egg per 1/4 cup coconut flour for structure

### The Resting Problem

Coconut flour continues absorbing moisture for 5-10 minutes after mixing. A batter that looks perfect at mixing will thicken significantly at rest. **Always let batter rest 5-10 minutes before judging consistency** -- then adjust liquid if needed.

### Combining with Almond Flour

The **3:1 ratio of almond flour to coconut flour** is the most commonly recommended blend:
- Coconut flour absorbs the excess oil from almond flour
- Almond flour provides the bulk and lighter texture
- Together they approximate wheat flour behavior better than either alone
- This ratio reduces the need for excessive eggs

Example: 1.5 cups almond flour + 0.5 cup coconut flour = ~2 cups flour blend.

### Stability in Dough

Research shows coconut flour in wheat dough increased dough development time and stability up to 20% substitution. At 30%, dough became unstable. In grain-free bread (no wheat), coconut flour's role is primarily as a moisture manager and fiber source, not a structural one.

**Sources**: [ScienceDirect: Dietary Fiber from Coconut Flour](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1466856406000452), [Panhandle Milling: Coconut Flour Guide](https://panhandlemilling.com/the-best-ultimate-guide-all-about-coconut-flour/), [Atkins: Baking with Coconut & Almond Flour](https://www.atkins.com/how-it-works/library/articles/low-carb-flour-baking-with-coconut-flour-almond-flour), [The Coconut Mama: Coconut vs Almond Flour](https://thecoconutmama.com/coconut-flour-vs-almond-flour-the-ultimate-verdict/)

## 5. Egg Whites vs Whole Eggs: The Rise Question

### The Science

Whipping egg whites incorporates air mechanically. The proteins (primarily ovalbumin) unfold at the air-water interface, creating a stable foam. When heated, these proteins coagulate around the air bubbles, setting the risen structure permanently.

**Whole eggs vs whites**:
- Whole eggs: Yolks contain fat, which INHIBITS foam formation. Fat molecules compete with proteins at the air-water interface, weakening the foam. Whole egg foams are less stable and lower volume.
- Egg whites only: No fat interference. Can be whipped to 6-8x their volume. More stable foam. Better rise.
- Yolks contribute: Flavor, color, emulsification (lecithin), and richness. Removing them entirely makes bread taste flat and pale.

### Best Approach for Grain-Free Bread

**Separate and recombine**:
1. Whip whites to stiff peaks with cream of tartar (1/8 tsp per 4 whites)
2. Mix yolks with other wet ingredients and the flour blend
3. Fold whites into batter in TWO additions:
   - First half: Mix more aggressively to lighten the heavy batter. Expect to lose ~50% of the volume.
   - Second half: Fold gently with a spatula. Preserve as much air as possible.

### Critical Technique Details

- **Room temperature eggs** whip to greater volume than cold eggs
- **Cream of tartar** stabilizes the foam -- egg white foam without stabilizer starts collapsing after 5 minutes
- **Speed matters**: Beat slowly at first (to create small, uniform bubbles), then increase speed
- **Timing is everything**: Once whites are whipped, fold immediately. Don't let them sit.
- **Don't over-beat**: Stiff peaks yes, dry peaks no. Over-beaten whites break into chunks when folded.
- **Use blanched almond flour**: Almond meal (with skins) is coarser and heavier, deflating foam faster.

### Reducing Eggy Flavor

If bread tastes too eggy:
- Use more whites than whole eggs (e.g., 2 whole eggs + 3 whites instead of 5 whole eggs)
- The whites contribute structure without the sulfur compounds that make bread taste "eggy"
- Let bread cool COMPLETELY before slicing -- egg flavor mellows significantly as bread cools
- Vanilla extract (if allowed) masks egg flavor

**Sources**: [Flavor Insider: Almond Flour Rising Guide](https://flavorinsider.com/12510/how-to-get-almond-flour-to-rise/), [Prepared Foods: Creating Egg White Foams](https://www.preparedfoods.com/articles/112210-creating-egg-white-foams), [Gluten Free on a Shoestring: Almond Flour Cake](https://glutenfreeonashoestring.com/almond-flour-cake/), [Pastry Chef Online: Egg Foam Method](https://pastrychefonline.com/the-egg-foam-method-thats-a-whole-different-cake/)

## 6. Apple Cider Vinegar + Baking Soda Leavening

### The Chemistry

Baking soda (sodium bicarbonate, NaHCO3) is a base. ACV is a 5% acetic acid solution. When combined:

NaHCO3 + CH3COOH -> CO2 + H2O + NaCH3COO

The CO2 gas provides lift. The reaction is FAST -- it begins immediately on contact.

### The Problem for Grain-Free Bread

1. **Reaction speed**: The acid-base reaction is nearly instantaneous. Most CO2 is produced in the first 1-2 minutes. If your batter isn't in the oven quickly, the gas escapes before the structure sets.
2. **No gluten to trap gas**: In wheat bread, gluten's elastic network traps CO2 bubbles. In almond flour batter, without psyllium or xanthan, gas just escapes upward.
3. **Soapy taste**: Excess baking soda (unreacted) leaves a metallic, soapy taste. This is a common complaint with GF bread.

### Optimal Ratio

- **Busby's Bakery** recommends: 1 teaspoon ACV per loaf (5g per 500g flour, or 1% baker's percentage)
- **For egg replacement**: 1 tsp baking soda + 1 tbsp vinegar replaces 1 egg
- **General rule**: 1/4 tsp baking soda per 1/2 cup acidic liquid
- **Critical**: More baking soda does NOT mean more rise. Excess just makes bread taste soapy. The acid must fully neutralize the soda.

### Making It Work Better

1. **Double-acting baking powder** is better than baking soda + ACV for grain-free bread. It releases CO2 in two phases: once when wet, once when heated. This gives you more time.
2. **Combine systems**: Use baking powder (1.5-2 tsp per cup flour) PLUS a small amount of ACV (1 tsp) for extra acid activation.
3. **Mix wet into dry quickly, get into oven fast**: The chemical reaction is time-sensitive. Delay = flat bread.
4. **Preheat the oven** -- never put GF bread into a cold oven.

### Vinegar's Other Benefits

Beyond leavening, ACV at 1% baker's percentage:
- Weakens protein bonds, creating a more tender crumb
- Lowers dough pH, which can improve flavor complexity
- Provides a subtle tanginess that masks eggy flavor

**Sources**: [Busby's Bakery: Vinegar for Baking](https://www.busbysbakery.com/vinegar-for-baking-bread-and-cakes/), [Food Republic: Vinegar in Bread Dough](https://www.foodrepublic.com/1750280/why-add-vinegar-bread-dough/), [The Fresh Loaf: Baking Powder + Vinegar in Bread](https://www.thefreshloaf.com/node/38480/using-both-baking-powder-and-vinegar-yeart-bread), [CySoda: Baking Soda for Bread](https://cysoda.com/article/how-to-use-baking-soda-to-make-bread)

## 7. Gums and Binders: Help or Hurt?

### Xanthan Gum

**How it works**: A polysaccharide produced by fermenting corn with Xanthomonas campestris bacteria. Forms long-chain polymers that create a gel-like matrix when hydrated. Traps gas bubbles during rise.

**Performance**: According to Art of Gluten-Free Baking (20+ years experience), xanthan gum "behaves most like gluten" of all replacers.

**Amounts for bread**:
- Sandwich/pan loaves: 1 tsp per cup flour
- Rustic loaves: 3/4 tsp per cup (allows larger crumb holes)
- Focaccia/pizza: 1.25 tsp per cup
- With high-protein flour blends (almond, chickpea): reduce by 25%

**ATK's verdict**: America's Test Kitchen says guar gum "does not perform as well as xanthan gum" for GF bread.

**Key insight**: The xanthan network creates discrete gas chambers (like a sponge), not continuous elastic sheets (like gluten's balloon). This is why GF bread crumb looks different even when well-made.

### Guar Gum

**How it works**: Derived from guar beans. Provides binding and thickening but significantly less elasticity than xanthan.

**Performance**: Creates a denser network -- good for sandwich bread that needs to hold together, but can make artisan bread heavy.

**Amounts**: Same as xanthan (1/4 tsp per cup non-yeasted, 1 tsp per cup yeasted) or up to 2:1 guar-to-xanthan if substituting.

### Psyllium vs Gums: The Verdict

| Property | Psyllium Husk | Xanthan Gum | Guar Gum |
|----------|--------------|-------------|----------|
| Elasticity | Excellent | Good | Poor |
| Gas trapping | Excellent | Good | Moderate |
| Moisture retention | Excellent | Moderate | Moderate |
| Shelf life impact | Extends significantly | Minimal | Minimal |
| Flavor impact | Slight (some brands) | None | None |
| Ease of use | Moderate (needs pre-hydration) | Easy | Easy |
| Amount needed | 5-6% flour weight | ~1% flour weight | ~1% flour weight |
| Best for | Bread, artisan loaves | All-purpose GF baking | Dense sandwich bread |

### The Combination Approach

Best results come from combining binders:
- **Psyllium (primary)**: Provides elasticity and moisture retention
- **Xanthan (secondary)**: Provides gas-trapping film
- **Tested ratio**: 3 parts psyllium to 2 parts xanthan gum

### Common Mistakes with Gums

1. **Too much = gummy texture** that's impossible to fix
2. **Improper hydration**: Gumminess usually comes from improper hydration, not wrong ratios
3. **Over-mixing**: Xanthan's fragile network breaks down with excessive mixing
4. **Old product**: Xanthan gum loses effectiveness after expiration date

**Sources**: [NogluBread: Xanthan Gum in GF Bread](https://www.noglubread.com/xanthan-gum-gluten-free-bread/), [Art of GF Baking: Gluten Replacers](https://artofglutenfreebaking.com/lets-talk-gluten-replacers-in-gluten-free-baking/), [Gluten Free Alchemist: Xanthan Gum Guide](https://www.glutenfreealchemist.com/xanthan-gum-and-gluten-free-baking-a-complete-guide/), [Zest for Baking: Guar vs Xanthan](https://zestforbaking.com/guar-gum-or-xanthan-gum-which-one-should-you-use/), [Hand and Heart GF: Xanthan & Psyllium Roles](https://handandheartglutenfree.com/xanthan-gum-psyllium-husk-role-in-gluten-free-baking/)

## 8. ATK / Serious Eats / King Arthur Insights

### America's Test Kitchen

ATK has published extensively on GF baking through their "How Can It Be Gluten Free" cookbook series (2 volumes). Key principles:

- **Custom flour blend**: ATK developed their own GF flour blend (rice flour base with starches) rather than using a single alternative flour. They did NOT tackle grain-free bread specifically -- their GF work uses rice, sorghum, tapioca, and potato starch.
- **Xanthan gum preferred**: ATK explicitly states guar gum underperforms compared to xanthan.
- **Oven proofing**: They use a simple oven proofing method for more consistent rise in GF breads.
- **Foil collars**: Used to control shape and size of GF baked goods.
- **Grain-free gap**: ATK has not published a dedicated grain-free (nut flour only) bread recipe. Their GF work is starch-based, which is a fundamentally different approach.

### King Arthur Baking

- **GF Bread Flour**: King Arthur makes a 1:1 GF bread flour substitute, but it's grain-based (sorghum, rice, tapioca). Not grain-free.
- **Key technique insight**: Their R&D team recommends adding 2-3 tablespoons extra liquid per cup of GF flour to compensate for higher absorption.
- **Maillard reaction science**: King Arthur's blog explains that browning requires amino acids + reducing sugars at 280F+, and notes that milk wash, egg wash, or higher temps can promote browning in any bread.
- **Grain-free gap**: Like ATK, King Arthur's GF work focuses on grain-based flour blends, not nut flours.

### Serious Eats

- **No dedicated grain-free bread article found**. Serious Eats has covered gluten-free baking and whole grains but does not appear to have published a rigorous, science-based treatment of grain-free (almond/coconut flour) bread.
- Their food science approach (Kenji Lopez-Alt style testing) has not been applied to this specific problem space.

### The Gap

**None of the Big Three (ATK, Serious Eats, King Arthur) have seriously tackled grain-free bread.** Their GF work relies on rice flour, sorghum, tapioca starch, and potato starch -- all grains or grain-derived starches. The almond flour + coconut flour + eggs problem space is largely the domain of paleo/keto food bloggers, not professional test kitchens. This means the "best practices" are derived from community testing, not rigorous controlled experiments (with the exception of the peer-reviewed psyllium research).

## 9. Common Mistakes and Why They Happen

### Mistake 1: Dense, Brick-Like Loaf
**Why**: Not enough leavening structure. Almond flour is heavy and can't trap air on its own.
**Fix**: Use whipped egg whites (not just whole eggs), add psyllium husk for gas-trapping, and use baking powder (not just baking soda).

### Mistake 2: Flat, No Rise
**Why**: Chemical leavening gas escaped before structure set. Batter sat too long before baking. Pan too large.
**Fix**: Preheat oven. Mix wet into dry quickly. Use an 8x4 loaf pan (not larger). Get batter into oven within 2-3 minutes of adding baking powder/soda.

### Mistake 3: Too Eggy
**Why**: Grain-free bread requires many eggs for structure, and without wheat flavor to balance, egg dominates.
**Fix**: Use more whites than whole eggs. Let bread cool completely before eating. Add 1 tsp ACV to cut egg flavor. Consider vanilla extract.

### Mistake 4: Cakey, Not Bread-Like
**Why**: Almond flour naturally produces cake-like texture due to high fat and no gluten.
**Fix**: Psyllium husk is the primary fix -- it adds chewiness and elasticity. Reduce any added fat. Use the 3:1 almond-to-coconut flour ratio so coconut flour absorbs excess oil.

### Mistake 5: Gummy or Wet Interior
**Why**: Under-baked (internal temp not reaching 200F+), too much psyllium, or coconut flour still absorbing moisture.
**Fix**: Bake until internal temp reaches 200-205F. Let batter rest 5-10 min after mixing so coconut flour fully hydrates. Reduce psyllium if gummy.

### Mistake 6: Crumbles Apart
**Why**: Not enough binding. Psyllium may be insufficient or improperly hydrated.
**Fix**: Pre-hydrate psyllium in warm water (10:1 ratio) for 5 min. Consider adding xanthan gum (1/2 tsp per cup flour) alongside psyllium. Ensure enough eggs.

### Mistake 7: No Crust Color
**Why**: No sugar for caramelization, insufficient protein at surface for Maillard.
**Fix**: Egg wash before baking. Start at 425F for 10 min, reduce to 350F. Consider a light dusting of baking soda on top for alkaline-promoted browning.

### Mistake 8: Soapy/Metallic Taste
**Why**: Too much baking soda, not enough acid to neutralize it.
**Fix**: Never exceed 1/4 tsp baking soda per cup flour. Always pair with equal or greater acid (ACV, cream of tartar, lemon juice). Switch to baking powder which is pre-balanced.

## 10. Synthesis: Optimal Approach Based on Science

### Flour Blend
- **3:1 almond flour to coconut flour** by volume
- Use **blanched, superfine almond flour** (not almond meal)
- Example: 1.5 cups (168g) almond flour + 0.5 cup (56g) coconut flour

### Binder System
- **Psyllium husk powder**: 2 tablespoons (14g), pre-hydrated in 6 tablespoons warm water for 5 minutes
- **Optional xanthan gum**: 1/2 teaspoon for additional gas-trapping (reduce psyllium slightly if using)

### Leavening System (Dual)
- **Mechanical**: Whip 4 egg whites to stiff peaks with 1/8 tsp cream of tartar
- **Chemical**: 1.5 tsp baking powder + 1 tsp ACV

### Liquid/Fat
- Egg yolks from the separated eggs (wet ingredient base)
- 2 tbsp melted butter or oil (minimal -- almond flour is already high-fat)
- 1 tsp ACV

### Technique
1. Preheat oven to 350F (some recipes start at 375-400F for crust)
2. Mix dry: almond flour, coconut flour, baking powder, salt
3. Hydrate psyllium separately in warm water (5 min)
4. Mix wet: egg yolks, melted fat, ACV, psyllium gel
5. Combine wet into dry
6. Rest batter 5 minutes (coconut flour hydration)
7. Whip egg whites with cream of tartar to stiff peaks
8. Fold whites into batter in 2 additions
9. Pour into greased 8x4 loaf pan
10. Optional: egg wash the top
11. Bake 45-55 min until internal temp reaches 200-205F
12. Cool IN THE PAN for 10 min, then on wire rack for 30+ min
13. Do not slice until completely cool

### Expected Result
A loaf that is:
- Taller than typical GF bread (whipped whites + psyllium gas-trapping)
- Chewy, not cakey (psyllium elasticity)
- Moist for 2-3 days (psyllium moisture retention + coconut flour fiber)
- Mild crust with some browning (egg wash + Maillard from almond protein)
- Less eggy (whites-heavy ratio + ACV)

This won't taste like wheat bread. It will taste like a well-made nut bread with good structure. The goal is the best possible version of what it is, not a wheat imitation.
